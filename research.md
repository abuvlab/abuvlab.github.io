My research lies at the intersection of statistical signal
processing, information theory, robust inference, machine
learning, and physically structured systems.

I develop methods that exploit geometric, algebraic, and
statistical structure to improve estimation, learning,
coding, and decoding under noise, model uncertainty,
and non-nominal operating conditions.

My work combines mathematical analysis with extensive
experience in real-time algorithm development, embedded
DSP, wireless communications, and engineering systems.

## Robust Statistical Estimation and Machine Learning

A central part of my research concerns estimation and
learning when conventional statistical assumptions are
violated by outliers, heavy-tailed disturbances, model
mismatch, or unreliable observations.

I investigate robust M-estimation, generalized
M-estimation, projection statistics, and methods for
detecting and limiting the influence of corrupted data.

### Robust Federated Learning

My work on robust complex-valued federated learning
investigates learning from distributed wireless clients
whose updates may be heterogeneous, corrupted, or
adversarial.

The research develops statistical methods for processing
complex-valued model updates and identifying anomalous
client contributions without relying exclusively on
conventional real-valued learning representations.

**Research status:** Manuscript submitted to
*IEEE Internet of Things Journal*.

### Robust Sensor Fusion and Trustworthy Autonomy

My work on robust fusion investigates graph residuals,
sparse corruption, coherent local shifts, and uncertainty
diagnostics in distributed sensing systems.

The objective is to distinguish isolated measurement
errors from structured disturbances that affect multiple
related sensors.

Related proposal development under **KVIST** addresses
assurance in autonomous systems, including robust
estimation, validation, uncertainty, and trustworthy
decision support under non-nominal operating conditions.

**Research status:** Robust sensor fusion manuscript
submitted to *IEEE Transactions on Signal and Information
Processing over Networks*. KVIST was developed as a
research funding proposal.

## Wireless Communications, RF Sensing, and Navigation

My wireless research combines robust statistical signal
processing with physically structured communication
channels, electromagnetic propagation, and practical
receiver implementation.

Research topics include reconfigurable intelligent
surfaces, polarization-space modulation, RF machine
learning, blind channel inference, satellite
communications, and navigation.

### Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces

My published research investigates communication methods
based on reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS),
including polarization-space modulation, media-based
modulation, blind equalization, and robust receiver
architectures.

These studies combine electromagnetic channel models
with statistical inference and complex-valued or
quaternion-valued neural networks.

**Selected publications:**

- *IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications*
  (2025): Robust media-based modulation with
  Eisenstein constellations.

- *IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation*
  (2025): Polarization-space modulation, robust
  estimation, and quaternion neural networks.

### Blind Channel Inference and Quantum Communications

**PRISM** investigates identifiable blind and semi-blind
channel inference for RIS-assisted TDD massive MIMO,
using structured phase probing, latent-data estimation,
and models incorporating partial observation and
uncertainty.

**VISTA** develops variational and structure-aware
inference methods for dual-polarization free-space
quantum communications, with attention to blind
equalization and non-nominal operating conditions.

**PRIOR** studies the effects of mismatched physical
priors on variational channel estimation, including
polarization mixing, fading, and alternative latent
statistical models.

**QUARTZ** develops robust quaternion-valued receiver
methods for polarization-diverse quantum communications,
including structured signal representations,
statistical robustness, and learning-based detection.

These are ongoing research and proposal activities;
their individual manuscript statuses are maintained
on the [Publications](/publications/) page.

### RF Machine Learning and Spectrum Awareness

My RF machine learning research includes hierarchical
signal classification, electronic intelligence,
time-frequency representations, and anomaly detection.

During my work at the Virginia Tech National Security
Institute, I investigated complex-valued signal
representations, conditional variational autoencoders,
and multistage processing of wideband RF observations.

The ongoing **MELD** manuscript addresses hierarchical
RF machine learning for spectrum situational awareness
in environments containing radar and communication
signals.

### Navigation and Distributed Sensing

My earlier research and industrial work includes GPS
signal acquisition, maximum-likelihood tracking,
time-of-arrival estimation, software-defined receivers,
and synchronization in mobile ad-hoc networks.

At NAVSYS, I led engineering work involving military GPS
signal processing and positioning under degraded
signal conditions.

The **KVANTELOS** research proposal extends these
interests toward quantum-enhanced inertial sensing,
classical inertial navigation, and robust asynchronous
quantum–classical state estimation in GNSS-denied
maritime environments.

## Information Geometry and Communication Theory

This research direction investigates how geometric
structure can be used to construct communication
representations and decoding methods with controlled
sensitivity to channel disturbances.

### Constant-Curvature Curve Tube Codes

My published work on constant-curvature curve tube
(C3T) codes develops geometric representations for
low-latency analog joint source–channel coding.

The approach uses structured curves in
higher-dimensional spaces to study the relationship
between local sensitivity, global curve separation,
and distortion under channel noise.

**Selected publications:**

- *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* (2023):
  Constant Curvature Curve Tube Codes for
  Low-Latency Analog Error Correction.

- *IEEE Transactions on Communications* (2024):
  Robust Constant Curvature Curve Communications
  with Complex and Quaternion Neural Networks.

### Multibranch Geometric Coding

**C3M** extends the single-curve approach to
multiple constant-curvature curves.

The research investigates branch selection,
source allocation, geometric separation,
and branch-conditioned decoding, together with
probabilistic and information-theoretic analysis.

**Research status:** Manuscript submitted to
*IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*.

## Quantum Information, Sensing, and Control

My quantum research investigates the relationship
between physical dynamics, quantum-state geometry,
measurement, control, and robust inference.

A recurring question is how useful geometric and
statistical information changes under realistic
noise, open-system dynamics, and measurement
constraints.

### Quantum Information Geometry — BERG

**BERG** investigates structured quantum-state
manifolds, intrinsic quantum geometry, and the
relationship between local and global
distinguishability.

The study examines quantum Fisher information,
trajectory design, channel-dependent geometry,
and distinguishability under noisy quantum
dynamics.

Particular attention is given to cases where
locally favorable geometric properties do not
necessarily produce globally distinguishable
quantum states.

**Research status:** Manuscript in preparation.

### Quantum Geometry for Robust Learning — BERG-QML

**BERG-QML** investigates how quantum information
geometry may inform the design and evaluation of
quantum kernel learning methods under noise.

The research direction connects structured quantum
feature representations, distinguishability,
and robustness in quantum learning systems.

**Research status:** Manuscript in preparation for
*Quantum Machine Intelligence*.

### Geometry-Aware Quantum Decoding

My published work with Stefan Werner develops
geometry-aware neural methods for low-latency
bosonic quantum decoding.

The approach uses structured spectral
representations, including Laplace–Beltrami and
Fourier modes, together with robust finite-shot
quantum-state reconstruction and neural models.

**Publication:** *Toward Robust Real-Time Bosonic
Decoding with Geometry-Aware Neural Networks*,
IEEE International Conference on Quantum Computing
and Engineering (QCE), 2026.

The related **CORRECT** research proposal develops
spectral-geometric approaches to bosonic quantum
error correction under realistic noise and
hardware constraints.

### Robust Quantum Control — TORQ

**TORQ** is a research proposal concerning robust
geometric methods for quantum control.

The proposed work combines Hamiltonian identification,
noise-aware estimation, calibration, and constrained
pulse optimization under uncertainty, drift,
crosstalk, leakage, and non-Gaussian disturbances.

### Quantum Sensing and Distributed Estimation

My quantum sensing research interests include
robust calibration, measurement uncertainty,
distributed sensing, and the integration of
quantum and classical observations.

These directions are represented in the
KVANTELOS and AFRQ-Sense research proposals.
